Starcrew

Industry:

Digital Platform

Project type:

Website Design

Role:

UI Design/Branding

Timeline:

2026

Problem:

Important campus events are often overlooked because information is spread across posters, emails, and multiple communication channels. Students struggle to keep track of opportunities that match their interests.

Solution:

Centralize event discovery into a single platform that surfaces relevant clubs and activities based on student interests, making campus involvement easier and more accessible.

Outcomes & Reflections

1. Usability Testing

Usability testing with SAIT students compared existing campus communication channels with the StarCrew prototype to identify barriers to discovering and joining campus events.

"Find and register for a club event this week that matches your interests."

Key Finding: Students struggled to find relevant events.

Participants relied on multiple channels to find an event.

  • Two students missed event emails because they were mixed with academic messages.
  • One student struggled to identify active events on social media and campus websites.
  • All participants reported feeling overwhelmed by the number of clubs and communication channels available.

2. Heuristic Evaluation

Recognition Over Recall

Students should not have to remember where an event was posted. StarCrew solves this by gathering club events, posters, and announcements into one place.

Visibility of System Status

Students need to know if an event is open, full, upcoming, or closing soon. Clear status labels help them understand what actions they can take.

Match Between System and Real World

Students search by interests, not only by club names. StarCrew organizes events by categories like design, networking, gaming, volunteering, and wellness.

3. Design Decisions

Based on usability testing and heuristic evaluation, the redesign makes campus events easier to find, filter, and understand by reducing scattered information.

I came up with 3 redesign decisions:

1. Centralized event discovery
2. Interest-based filtering
3. Clear event details

4. Final Design

The final StarCrew website design uses usability testing and heuristic evaluation findings to help SAIT students discover clubs, events, and information in one platform.

Final Outcome

The final StarCrew website addresses the challenge of fragmented campus communication by providing a single platform for club discovery and event promotion. Students can easily browse clubs, view upcoming events, and access relevant information without searching through multiple channels. The result is a more engaging and accessible experience that encourages greater participation in campus activities.
