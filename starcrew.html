Starcrew

Industry:

Digital Platform

Project type:

Website Design

Role:

UI Design/Branding

Timeline:

2026

Problem:

Important campus events are often overlooked because information is spread across posters, emails, and multiple communication channels. Students struggle to keep track of opportunities that match their interests.

Solution:

Centralize event discovery into a single platform that surfaces relevant clubs and activities based on student interests, making campus involvement easier and more accessible.

Outcomes & Reflections

1. Usability Testing

Usability testing sessions were conducted with SAIT students using observation-based testing. Participants were asked to complete a task using existing campus communication channels and then interact with the StarCrew prototype. The goal was to understand how students currently discover campus events and identify barriers that prevent participation.

“You want to find a student club event happening this week that matches your interests. Use the available campus resources to find one and register for it.”

Key Findings: Students struggled to discover relevant events efficiently.

On their first attempt, all participants relied on multiple communication channels before finding an event:

  • Two students checked their email inboxes but overlooked event announcements because they were mixed with academic and administrative messages.
  • One student searched social media pages and campus websites but found it difficult to determine which events were currently active and open for registration.
  • All participants reported feeling overwhelmed by the number of clubs and communication channels available.

2. Heuristic Evaluation

Recognition Over Recall

Students should not have to remember where an event was posted. StarCrew solves this by gathering club events, posters, and announcements into one place.

Visibility of System Status

Students need to know if an event is open, full, upcoming, or closing soon. Clear status labels help them understand what actions they can take.

Match Between System and Real World

Students search by interests, not only by club names. StarCrew organizes events by categories like design, networking, gaming, volunteering, and wellness.

3. Design Decisions

Based on the usability testing and heuristic evaluation, the redesign focuses on making campus events easier to discover, filter, and understand. The main goal is to reduce confusion caused by scattered event information across posters, emails, and social media.

I came up with 3 redesign decisions:

1. Centralized event discovery
2. Interest-based filtering
3. Clear event details

4. Final Design

I translated the findings from usability testing and heuristic evaluation into a final website design for StarCrew. The prototype focuses on helping SAIT students discover clubs, explore events, and access club information in one clear platform.

Final Outcome

The final StarCrew website addresses the challenge of fragmented campus communication by providing a single platform for club discovery and event promotion. Students can easily browse clubs, view upcoming events, and access relevant information without searching through multiple channels. The result is a more engaging and accessible experience that encourages greater participation in campus activities.
